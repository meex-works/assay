How it works

Assay Office is the public end of assay, an open curation system for pump.fun coins. It doesn't post good coins. It posts pure launches: coins whose structure is honest at launch. Whether a coin is worth anything is for you to find out.

Seeing every launch

Two independent feeds watch pump.fun: a gRPC stream of every successful pump transaction and a websocket on launches only. Each launch is checked against both, and anything only one of them saw is logged as a gap.

The checklist, at 0:30

  • Dev buy: the creator bought 5% of supply or less, and still holds it.
  • No bundle: no group among the first buyers that was funded by the creator, funded by one wallet, created in the last hour and bought in the launch's first slots, or funded within minutes of each other, holding 3% or more.
  • No known bad actors: no early buyers funded by a wallet that has funded early buyers in other coins.
  • Clean creator: not a creator, or a signing wallet, with several other launches in the last day.
  • Original: no earlier coin with the same metadata, the same image, or the same name or ticker in the last day.
  • Honest links and config: linked accounts exist, and any promise of holder rewards matches the launch's settings.
  • Alive: at least ten buyers from independent funding sources. This only keeps the channel readable; it's never shown.

pump.fun's own mayhem agent, which buys most mayhem coins in their first seconds, is known by address and never counts against a coin.

Dead on arrival

Most coins never leave the bonding curve's floor, or fall back to it within minutes. A coin with fewer than three outside buyers at 0:30, or a market cap under $2.75K, is dead and isn't checked further. A posted coin that falls under $2.75K is edited to say so, and its watch ends. The floor is fixed in SOL, so its dollar value moves with SOL's price: about $2,600 at $93.

The watch, for fifteen minutes

Every trade on a posted coin is read as it lands. If a late bundle buys in, if the dev sells, or if the coin is pumped by wallets that aren't independent, the post is edited to say when and why, and the event goes into the coin's thread. Coins that hold get "Still pure at 5:00" and "Still pure at 15:00".

The comparison set

One pure coin in five is watched exactly the same way but never posted. Comparing the two answers a question the channel has to face: does posting a coin draw the bundles that kill it?

What assay doesn't do

No prices, no market caps, no predictions, no recommendations. No contracts, no wallets, no signing. The weights and thresholds are public, in pure.yaml, and every claim about pump.fun's mechanics was checked against mainnet.
